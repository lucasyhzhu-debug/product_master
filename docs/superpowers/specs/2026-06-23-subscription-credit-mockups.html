① Flow ② Weekly cycle ③ CRM record ④ Agreement ⑤ Order history ⑥ Schedule calendar ⑦ Visual invoice ⑧ Midweek top-up ⑨ Drawdown ⑩ Out-of-credit ⑪ Telegram

Mockups for the Frollie subscription & credit feature, modelled on the Frollie × Amsterdamn Cafe agreement (baseline 1,050 pcs/wk · Rp 29,000/pc · Rp 30,450,000 prepaid Mondays · 150/day by 09:00). Usually one product per day, but each day can carry multiple products — the day subtotals always sum to the planned weekly credit. Figures illustrative.

① The big picture

One value flows through everything

The confirmed schedule (per day, per product) computes the invoice, which funds the week's credit pool, which orders draw down, ending in reconciliation. Schedule = invoice = credit, always aligned.

Midweek top-up invoices add credit to the pool without touching the schedule (section ⑦).

⑧ Flexibility

Midweek top-up — driven by the schedule, not a separate form

No standalone "top-up" entry point. You just edit this week's schedule (add days/qty/products). If the new total exceeds what's already funded, the system recognises it and walks you through the same confirm → invoice journey, flagged as a top-up.

Same journey, flagged as top-up
1
Open the current week's schedule calendar, add the extra delivery / bump a day's qty.
2
New week total > funded credit → system prompts (see right).
3
Confirm → generates a top-up invoice for only the delta, flagged "top-up" in the invoice + ledger + flows.
4
On paid → +delta ledger entry; the new order(s) draw from the larger pool.

⑨ Consumption

Credit drawdown — delivered (solid) vs. planned (dashed), on the customer dashboard

Lives on the customer dashboard, aggregating all of that customer's subscriptions for the week. Bars = pcs/day (left axis); line = credit left in Rp (right axis). Solid = delivered, dashed & lighter = still planned, so you can see what's left to ship and whether it will consume the credit. If the projected line won't reach zero (they under-ordered), it flags leftover credit.

Solid = delivered through today (Wed); dashed = the rest of the week's plan. Aggregates every subscription the customer holds that week.

⑩ Edge case

Out of credit → flag, split, re-invoice (two paths)

Two distinct situations. Scheduled subscription orders (only editable in the scheduler) split and re-invoice. Ad-hoc orders for the same customer apply partial credit + collect the remainder. Either way the shortfall is always flagged first.

Path A — scheduled order short on credit 🔒 scheduler
1
A planned day can't be fully covered (earlier ad-hoc use drained the pool).
2
Flag partial fulfilment on that day in the scheduler.
3
Split the order: bill only the credit-covered portion against remaining credit.
4
The remainder + rest-of-week plan becomes a new invoice sent to the customer (same top-up journey, section ⑧).
Keeps the single edit path: the split happens in the scheduler, never on the kanban.
